Free, for good

Linux and GreyHaven cost nothing. No licence to buy, no subscription, no trial that runs out.

What could you keep?

$
Keep your PC and switch to Linux, and you keep $500 you'd have spent on a new one.

Other costs, from Microsoft: one more year of Windows 10 security updates is $30 a PC (until 12 October 2027), and a Windows 11 Home licence is $139 if you build a PC yourself. Upgrading a PC that qualifies for Windows 11 is free.

No surprise restarts

Security updates install quietly, and Linux doesn't restart your PC in the middle of your work. You restart when it suits you.

No ads, no account

No ads in your menus and no online account needed to sign in. Sharing data with the makers is something you opt into, not out of.

New life for older PCs

Linux runs on most PCs that Windows 11 turns away, including ones without the newer security chip (TPM 2.0) or a recent processor.

Your PC, your rules

Nothing gets installed that you didn't ask for. Thousands of free apps are a click away in the app store, and you can change anything.

    Why people make the switch

    Windows 10 support has ended

    Over a year since support ended

    Microsoft stopped updating Windows 10 on 14 October 2025. Security updates now only come through Extended Security Updates, which end on 12 October 2027.

    Source: Microsoft, Windows 10 Extended Security Updates

    • "This PC can't run Windows 11." Plenty of good PCs don't meet its requirements.
    • Ads and nags. Suggestions in the Start menu, prompts to sign in, offers to try things.
    • Updates on someone else's schedule. Restarts when you least expect them.
    • Privacy. More of what you do sent online than people are comfortable with.
    • A PC that's getting slower with every year of updates.

    So why doesn't everyone switch? Mostly, it's not knowing whether their PC will work with Linux. That's the first thing GreyHaven checks.

    How it works

    1. 1

      Download

      Install GreyHaven like any app. It's free.

    2. 2

      Scan your PC

      It checks every part against Linux's own driver lists, in about a minute. Nothing on your PC changes.

    3. 3

      Pick your Linux

      Ubuntu, Linux Mint or Pop!_OS: it suggests the one that fits your PC, with plain pros and cons.

    4. 4

      Switch, step by step

      It makes your USB stick, puts the next steps on your phone, and restarts your PC into Linux to try it first.

    Sometimes there are two good paths

    Questions

    Will this delete my files?

    Not if you choose Keep Windows too: Windows, your apps and your files stay as they are, and Linux gets space next to them. GreyHaven still asks you to back up first, because changing the drive's layout is the one step that can go wrong. Replace Windows erases the drive, and GreyHaven won't let you go on until your backup checklist is done. Scanning never changes anything.

    Can I go back to Windows?

    Yes. You try Linux from the USB stick before installing anything; take the stick out and Windows starts as before. With Keep Windows too, you choose Windows or Linux every time your PC starts.

    Will my games work?

    Most single-player games do, through Proton in Steam. Some online games block Linux with their anti-cheat. GreyHaven checks the games on your PC, and you can try any game in the checker above.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. GreyHaven is free and open source, with no ads, no account and no paid version. Ubuntu, Linux Mint and Pop!_OS are free too.

    Do I need to be technical?

    No. GreyHaven is written for people who've only ever used Windows. Every step is explained in plain words with pictures, it checks your PC before each step, and your guide goes with you on your phone when your PC restarts.
